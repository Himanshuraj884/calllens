# User research guide

Run 5 interviews of 20 minutes each, before and after the pilot. Take notes in the table at the bottom. Ask about real past behaviour, not hypotheticals.

## Who to talk to
People who make calls to customers or recruiters: classmates who did sales or outreach internships, tech-fest sponsorship teammates, debate club members who handle outreach. Ideal: anyone who writes follow-ups after calls.

## Before the pilot (problem interviews)
1. Walk me through the last call you had with a customer or sponsor. What did you do right after it?
2. How do you remember what was agreed? Where do you write it down?
3. What gets missed or forgotten most often?
4. What tools do you use today for notes or follow-ups? What do you dislike about them?
5. If a tool could do one thing after your calls, what would it be?

## After the pilot (usability interviews)
1. Which part of the result did you read first? Which did you skip?
2. Was anything in the summary wrong or missing? Show me.
3. Did you trust the action items? What would make you trust them more?
4. Would you use this again next week? What would stop you?
5. What is the one change that would make this clearly worth using?

## Rules
- Do not pitch. Do not lead ("wouldn't it be useful if...").
- Ask "why" and "can you show me" often.
- Get consent before recording any call or interview.

## Notes table (copy per interview)
| Participant | Role | Date | Key quote | Pain point | Insight | Product change |
|-------------|------|------|-----------|-----------|---------|----------------|

## Synthesis
Group notes into 3 to 5 themes. For each theme write: what you heard (with counts, e.g. "4 of 5"), what you will change, and what you will not change and why.
